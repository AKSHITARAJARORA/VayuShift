Everyone has tried to pay farmers not to burn. The math kept saying burn.

02 — why it failed

A biomass plant pays roughly ₹1,500–2,000 per tonne for stubble. Trucking it there costs ₹10–15 per tonne, per kilometre. Run those two numbers against each other at 40 km and the old model breaks. Aggregate into full truckloads and route it well, and the same 40 km stays profitable.

We don't move stubble. We move information about where it already is.

03 — the solution

VayuShift is a matching engine, not a trucking company. It combines three public datasets to see, before anyone burns anything, exactly which fields have surplus stubble, which plants need it, and which route between them still leaves a profit.

DATASET 01

NASA FIRMS

Real-time satellite fire-hotspot data flags exactly where and when stubble is being burned — and, read the right way, where it's about to be.

DATASET 02

MNRE directories

Public records of every biomass power plant's location and intake capacity, so we know who's buying, how much, and how close they are.

DATASET 03

Agricultural Census

District-level residue density tells us where stubble is concentrated enough to fill a truck without driving farm to farm to do it.

How a tonne of stubble finds a buyer

Four steps, run automatically every burning season.

Farmer lists on WhatsApp

No app, no login, no literacy barrier. A farmer messages a WhatsApp bot with village, quantity, and moisture — the same way they'd message a relative.

We cluster nearby listings

Individual fields rarely fill a truck. The engine groups listings within a village cluster until they add up to a full, economical load.

We route to the plant that clears a margin

Cross-referencing MNRE plant capacity and live fire-hotspot pressure, the engine picks the buyer and route that leaves a guaranteed positive payout — before a truck ever moves.

Buyer pays, farmer is paid

The biomass plant pays for the load. Transport is deducted at the source. The remainder reaches the farmer or aggregator — the same week, not the same season.

Farmers never pay us. Plants do.
